# UBP Partner Tiering Framework — Financial Structures & Incentives

**Confidential — internal reference.** Captured from the Finance team's working-session decks (`UBP_Partner_Tiering_Framework_v2.pptx` / `v3_simplified.pptx`, Wami — Finance Lead, Sept 2026). This is the authoritative source for **task #40** (coach/partner payment-split) — supersedes any earlier guessed numbers. Covers **all marketplace partners**, not just Elevation coaches: Lifestyle Blueprint businesses and coaches sit under one unified tiering system.

---

## The core idea, in one breath

Every partner is placed into a tier by what they actually **are** (Size, Service, Pricing) — not what they pay to get there. Within that tier, they choose between a fixed, low-commission rent (Option A) or a flexible, zero-rent revenue share (Option B). A separate, timing-based badge rewards everyone who joins before public launch, regardless of tier or which option they picked. Three different questions, three different mechanisms, kept deliberately separate so each stays honest and defensible on its own terms.

## Tier structure — Access / Plus / Signature

A joint judgment from three inputs — no single one decides the tier alone:
- **Size** — scale of the operation
- **Service** — what they actually deliver (single-service vs. multi-service/wider reach)
- **Pricing** — what they charge their own customers

**Signature is deliberately empty by design right now** — no real Signature-scale partner exists yet to calibrate rent/commission against. Don't guess numbers and retrofit later; wait for a real candidate.

## Payment options — locked numbers

Same two-option structure at every tier:

| Tier | Option A — Fixed | Option B — Unbounded |
|---|---|---|
| Access | **€15/mo + 10% commission** | €0/mo + negotiated %, uncapped, case-by-case |
| Plus | **€45/mo + 8% commission** | €0/mo + negotiated %, uncapped, case-by-case |
| Signature | TBD | Negotiated |

- **Option A** suits a partner betting on their own volume: predictable cost, lower marginal rate.
- **Option B** is deliberately unbounded, not bounded to a range — partners in the same tier can be at genuinely different stages, and forcing them onto one comparison band would under- or over-charge someone. This is a deliberate rejection of a bounded-range compromise, not a hedge — but it's **only defensible with a documented, consistent rationale behind every negotiated rate** (see Open Decisions below).

## Founding Partner Badge — timing, not spend

- Universal to anyone live before public launch, regardless of tier or Option A/B.
- Decoupled entirely from tier and payment option — an Access partner and a future Signature partner earn it the same way.
- The whole rule: join before public launch, keep it.
- Marketed honestly as "you were here first," never "you paid more."
- **Explicitly excluded:** Roomy.ie and Course Compass — these are third-party platform/tool integrations, not marketplace partners, and sit outside the tiering framework and the badge entirely.

## Benchmark — why this design

Compared against Shopify Partners (earned via revenue/retention/certifications, never bought), Amazon Associates (commission-only, tiered by volume), Airbnb Superhost (awarded status, top ~10%, guest-facing metrics), Twitch Partner (aspirational status word). **UBP is the most direct combination**: earned tier placement like Shopify/Superhost, *plus* a genuine A/B risk-profile choice none of the four offer, *plus* a timing badge fully separate from tier status.

## Marketing cornerstones (why it's trustworthy)

1. **Status names, not bookkeeping labels** — Access/Plus/Signature, not "Tier 1/2/3," so it's a name a partner can *be*.
2. **Earned placement, never bought** — classification from Size/Service/Pricing, not a fee buying a higher label.
3. **Benefit-led, not feature-led** — pitch is "get discovered by students actively looking for a deal like yours," not "€15/month, 10% commission."
4. **Real scarcity, not manufactured** — the Founding window genuinely closes at public launch. No fake countdowns stacked on top of a real deadline.
5. **A visible, growing roster is proof** — 25 live partners, 29 confirmed and queued, 15 live coaches (as of Sept 2026). Not a cold-start pitch.

## Incentive design (why the incentives actually work)

1. **Two options beat one price** — a flat fee limits downside, a revenue share limits upside risk; offering both turns a pricing decision UBP would make *for* a partner into a risk-preference choice the partner makes *for themselves*.
2. **Option B needs documentation discipline** — the single most load-bearing point for Finance: the unbounded reasoning is sound but only stays defensible with a documented, consistent rationale behind every negotiated rate, or it reads as favoritism if partners compare notes.
3. **Non-monetary incentives, already built** — real Partner Portal views/claims data ("40 members claimed this deal last month") is a genuine co-marketing asset that costs nothing new to produce.
4. **Tier mobility is a promotion, not a bill** — moving up leads with the achievement, with advance notice and the same first-pick-of-option treatment given at launch.
5. **Founding badge rewards timing, not spend.**
6. **Risk-reversal belongs in the pitch** — "no bill until launch, and you choose your terms before we do" is a stronger opening line than the tier structure itself, named and marketed explicitly.

## The honest risk case — six open items Finance owns (not weaknesses in the research)

1. **Exact tier thresholds are undefined** — the method (Size/Service/Pricing) is locked, the actual Access/Plus (and eventually Signature) boundaries aren't.
2. **Review cadence is undecided** — how often, and by whom, a partner's tier gets reassessed.
3. **Option B fairness is the single biggest risk in the whole framework** — who has authority to negotiate rates, and how they're reviewed for internal consistency.
4. **Signature tier calibration** — real numbers wait for real Signature-scale candidates, not guessed now.
5. **A/B revenue-balance assumption is unverified** — asserted as roughly equivalent for UBP, not yet stress-tested against real Access/Plus partner revenue.
6. **Existing-partner transition has no owner or timeline** — the retroactive sort and grace window are agreed in principle, not assigned.

## Implementation flow — Outreach → Marketing → Finance → Tech, Legal gating every stage in parallel

- **Outreach** (first): provisional tier read + benefit-led pitch. Needs a documented, consistent tier-assessment method so two people don't guess two different tiers for a similar business.
- **Marketing** (second): portal messaging to existing partners can move early; public marketing waits until Finance's billing mechanism is genuinely live. Every public claim checked against Finance before publishing, not after.
- **Finance** (third): billing cadence + system of record — the single most important requirement in the whole framework. Natural default cadence is **monthly**, matching Option A. Without a real system of record, Option B's fairness risk becomes tribal knowledge instead of something defensible.
- **Tech** (fourth): fields, storage, admin tooling at scale — can't be built before Finance's requirements are locked (risks a rebuild), but can't wait until last either (nothing scales past a handful of manually-tracked partners without it).
- **Legal** (parallel, gates every stage above): agreement templates drafted early, alongside Outreach — no partner-facing step is safe until there's something real for the partner to sign. Also covers a genuine open question on Irish contract law since some UBP team members are themselves minors.

## What's already built (real head start, not a gap)

- `partners` table — existing category/type fields already backing the live marketplace
- `partner_users` + RLS — links a business-role login to its own partner record, sees only its own data
- `partner_performance_stats` — real, working view/claim/unique-engaged-user tracking, already live
- `partner_category_benchmark()` — already computes average/median views and claims per category
- `commission_declarations` + `partner_payouts` — finance-dashboard functions already role-gated to finance/founder/operations
- Role system (`founder, finance, operations, business, coach, handler`) — tier-admin tooling can plug straight in, no new permissions to invent

**Genuinely net-new needed:** a tier field, a payment-option field, structured negotiated-rate storage restricted to Finance/Founder, Partner Portal display of a partner's own tier/badge/terms, admin tooling to assign and log tier changes, and the billing/invoicing mechanism itself once Finance's cadence and revenue-definition questions are settled.
